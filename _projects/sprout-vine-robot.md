Soft growing ("vine") robots extend from their tip by everting material, which lets them push through rubble and confined spaces where rigid robots and people cannot easily go. At MIT Lincoln Laboratory, I worked on making these robots practical for emergency responders: faster growth, steering, shape sensing, and integrated sensor payloads.

On **SPROUT** (Soft Pathfinding Robotic Observation Unit), I redesigned the soft body to use 70% less fabric and deploy three times faster, integrated sensing and actuation around a tip-mounted camera, and built modular test infrastructure for benchmarking individual components and the integrated system. With sensor specialists, I helped integrate shape sensing and acoustic localization into the platform. Related work includes a high-speed tip mount (**Gotta Grow Fast**, IEEE RA-L 2026), studies of vine robot steerability, and shape sensing with distributed IMUs (BioRob 2026).

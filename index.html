Antonio Alvarez Valdivia

Postdoctoral Research Associate · Northeastern University

Northeastern University

Boston, Massachusetts

I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate at Northeastern University, working with Prof. Kris Dorsey on sensing-and-actuating wearable garments. My research seeks to make physical interaction programmable: I design soft and reconfigurable robotic systems, then use sensing, modeling, and learning to adapt how they communicate, sense, and act as the user, task, or environment changes.

Previously, I was a Postdoctoral Researcher at MIT Lincoln Laboratory with Dr. Nathaniel Hanson, where I developed soft growing (vine) robots for search and rescue and field deployment, and served as PI on the ICE-EARS program. I received my Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering from Purdue University in 2025, advised by Prof. Laura H. Blumenschein, where my dissertation focused on the design, control, and usability of ubiquitous haptic interfaces for human–machine interaction. Before that, I earned my B.S. in Mechanical Engineering from Iowa State University.

Research focus

  1. Human–robot interaction: how robots can communicate what they have learned, and how people perceive and use the feedback they receive.
  2. Soft haptic interfaces: pneumatic, wearable, and robot-mounted displays that communicate information to people, from fluidic-logic control to personalized signal design.
  3. Field-deployable soft robots: vine robots with integrated sensing for search and rescue, confined spaces, and distributed sensor deployment.
  4. Accessible soft robot design: open-source hardware, reproducible fabrication, and benchmarking for soft robotic systems.

Research

Soft, human-centered robots that work with people and in the real world.

Robotic and human-machine systems increasingly adapt how they interpret information and make decisions, yet the physical interfaces through which they sense, communicate, and act often remain fixed. My research seeks to make physical interaction programmable. I design soft and reconfigurable robotic systems with a range of possible shapes, stiffnesses, contact behaviors, and feedback modalities, then use sensing, modeling, and learning to select among those behaviors as the user, task, or environment changes. I view this closed loop as physical AI grounded in embodiment: the body defines which interactions are physically possible, while sensing, models, and learning determine which response suits the moment.

Research directions

1 · Adaptive haptic & immersive interfaces

Interfaces that adapt how task-relevant information is spread across haptic, visual, and immersive channels. Soft, modular, reconfigurable hardware expands what an interface can physically render, and information-theoretic methods match those signals to each user and task, toward personalized, co-adaptive feedback.

2 · Human-aware wearable interfaces

Body-worn systems that integrate sensing, feedback, and reconfigurable mechanical support in one platform. They estimate human and task state, communicate what a robot has learned, guide action, and change their mechanics when feedback alone isn't enough, for teleoperation, occupational safety, and care.

3 · Embodied adaptation in soft robots

Compliant, reconfigurable robots (growing and continuum robots, grippers, deployable tools) that stay soft where deformation helps and stiffen where the task demands it. Distributed sensing lets them understand their own bodies and contacts, and use their bodies to gather information.
